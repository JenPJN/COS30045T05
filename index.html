Energy at home: what our TVs use, and what that power costs

Televisions are one of the most common appliances in Australian homes, and every model sold carries an energy rating label. The first three charts explore those labels: how much energy TVs use, how screen size drives consumption, and how the major screen technologies compare. The final chart puts that consumption in context by showing how wholesale electricity prices in the National Electricity Market have changed since 1998.

Hover over any chart for exact values. Click legend items to show or hide a series.

Half of all TV models use under 400 kWh a year

Distribution of labelled energy consumption across all 3,703 TV models in the dataset. Each bar counts the models in a 50 kWh/year band. A small number of very large screens stretch the tail past 1,000 kWh.

OLED screens use the most energy

Mean labelled energy consumption by screen technology. Switch to 55-inch TVs to compare like-for-like: the gap narrows because OLED models skew towards larger screens.

Bigger screens, bigger energy bills

Each circle is a group of models from one brand with the same screen technology and size. Circle area shows how many models are in the group. Consumption rises steadily with screen size, with OLED models sitting towards the top at any given size.

Wholesale electricity prices have more than tripled since 2011

Annual average spot price ($ per megawatt hour) by NEM region, 1998–2024. The dashed line is the mainland average (Queensland, NSW, Victoria and South Australia). Tasmania joined the market in 2005 and the Snowy region was abolished in 2008.

Data: Australian Government Energy Rating (TV labels) and Australian Energy Regulator (spot prices), supplied as COS30045 Exercise 5 datasets.
